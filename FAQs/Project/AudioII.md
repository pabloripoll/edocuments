Basic of Sound Digitalization and Recognition
An audio signal is a representation of sound, typically using either a changing level of electrical voltage for analogue signals and trespassed to digital by a series of binary numbers for its signals.
From an audio sample it is possible to obtain different visual representation of it to be represented in a specific visual way for a particular analysis.
Among the options we can obtain the representation of the amplitude of the signal passing through 0 DB between a determined threshold on a timeline, resulting in a graph with the different amplitudes known as graphic envelope.


Filtering The same audio divided in given frequency (x) bands range will results in (x) graphic envelope representations. 
Putting all together in a same graphic creates an advanced representation known as audio frequency spectrum.
 
In the above graphic, frequencies are represented in comparison with light spectrum. The lower frequencies will be represented towards infra-red spectrum and the higher the frequency towards ultra-violet.

Analysing multiple sounds, it can be seen that depending on the source it performs a different spectrum behaviour for it is known as the tone.
The tone of a sound is the quality by virtue of which we can distinguish two sounds of equal frequency and intensity emitted by two different sound sources. Tone is due to the fact that a sound is generally not pure and depends mainly on the spectrum.
All tone fingerprint has its identifiable behaviour attack, sustain, decay and release.
•	Attack represents the time it takes to rise from an initial value of zero to its maximum level. 
•	Sustain is the time during which it remains at this level. 
•	Decay is the time for the initial falling off to the sustain level. 
•	Release is the time it takes to move from the sustain to its final level.

So, the tone fingerprint of the sound allows us to recognize what is listening, if it is the sound of surrounding elements or voices of different people, whether if they are speaking in our language or a different one, but none of these could be processed for our brain if has not something to compare and there is when it comes our Auditory Memory.

Back to human speech, any letter has a fingerprint but a combination of letters creates another recognizable fingerprint.
In an ideal (cleanest) audio sample belonging to a human speech, we will be able to identify a typical envelope for each syllable recorded or for an entire word.
 
From the above graphic is observable different kind of registers accumulation through time of the envelope corresponding to a given letter pronunciation.

Analysis of Dunstan Study
According to the Dunstan study, new born babies up to 3 months of age, tend to modulate all vowels and primarily the consonants H (carry-over) and N in an attempt to communicate according to their needs.
This method claims to have identified that these modulated sounds contain phonetic patterns whose sounds and meanings correspond to the following identifiers:
•	Eh – Upper wind (burp)
•	Eairh – Lower wind (gas)
•	Heh – Disconfort (hot, cold, wet)
•	Neh – Hunger
•	Owh – Sleepness

These five words identified by Dunstan Study is for baby caregivers could train their listening to these sound fingerprints so can attend accordingly to the real baby needs.
As has been explained before, the method is a listening training with all the params from the sound has to be saved in our auditory memory and frequency range can be take it into account to distinguish whether if it is a baby that is crying or it is generated by a different source.

Opening the babies crying samples, it can be identified what Dunstan Study suggests.

Neh sound


The characterization of the Neh sound is that is has a rapid but low attack amplitude, with great energy gather in the lower frequency of a baby tone, a mid-sustain because of the E sound, long decay and short release.
Eh sound

The characterization of the Eh sound is that is has a rapid attack and amplitude, with a short sustain because on the E sound, and short decay / release.

Eairh sound

The Eairh sound is that is has a rapid but low attack amplitude, with great energy gather in the lower frequency of a baby tone, a mid-sustain because of the E sound, and long decay.

Owh sound

The characterization of the Owh sound is that is has a slow low attack on lower frequency, almost no sustain but long decay and release.

Heh sound
The Heh sound has the particularity of having a remarkable hissing before a rapid attack, not much sustain, and a rapid both decay and release.
